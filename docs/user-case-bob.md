# User Case Story: Bob the Builder Gets Ahead of Every Site Visit

Bob—known around the office as “Bob the Builder”—is a 42-year-old operations manager at a growing civil construction company in Victoria. He has spent nearly two decades in construction, starting on the tools before moving into site coordination and estimating.

Bob is dependable, hands-on, and proud of getting jobs done properly. He is also a husband and father of two young children. His day starts early, often before the family is awake, and ends late when site visits, supplier calls, quotes, and unexpected problems pile up. He wants to be home for dinner and his children’s activities, but a “quick look at a site” can easily become a two-hour drive, a missed appointment, and another evening spent catching up on paperwork.

Every new job adds pressure. A client calls about trenching near a busy road and needs a quote quickly. Bob has an address and a few details in a text message, but before he can price the work or send a crew, he needs to know what is really waiting at the site.

Is there underground infrastructure? Does the work require a permit? Will traffic management be needed? Is the location accessible for plant and equipment? Are there risks that will delay the job or make the quote inaccurate?

Today, those answers are scattered. Bob checks maps, searches council websites, looks through emails, recalls what happened on similar jobs, and calls external bodies. Some details are in his head; others depend on whether he has time to track them down. When the day gets busy, a check can be forgotten or postponed.

The result is often another site visit.

Bob drives across town, sometimes in traffic, only to find that the job needs a permit, BYDA clearance is still outstanding, or the crew cannot access the area as expected. The company loses time, the quote needs to change, and Bob has to explain the delay to both the client and his team. Worse, missing a critical check before excavation can expose the business to serious safety, cost, and scheduling risks.

With Site Ahead, Bob enters the job address, chooses “Excavation & Trenching,” and pastes the client’s message: “The trench needs to run near the back fence beside a busy road.”

Site Ahead immediately creates a job-specific pre-visit checklist. It automatically checks relevant traffic conditions, air quality, and whether a road-reserve permit is likely required. It recognises that the work is near a boundary and adds the appropriate risk check.

The system then separates the work into clear next steps:

- Automated checks are completed immediately, with results Bob can review.
- Third-party requests, such as BYDA clearance and road-occupancy permits, are drafted and ready for Bob to review and send.
- On-site checks, such as access conditions and dust-control requirements, are clearly assigned to the person visiting the site.

Instead of relying on memory, emails, and rushed research, Bob sees one live view of the job: what is already known, what is awaiting a reply, what needs approval, and what must be checked in person.

This means Bob can make a better decision before anyone gets in a vehicle. If a site visit is still necessary, the team arrives prepared and knows exactly what to inspect. If it is not necessary yet, Bob saves the trip and can spend that time finalising quotes, coordinating work, or getting home on time.

For Bob the Builder, Site Ahead is more than a checklist. It is a way to protect his time, reduce wasted site visits, avoid expensive surprises, and give every crew a clearer picture of the job before they arrive.
